## How much power does a 3,000 watt inverter really supply?

A 3,000 watt RV inverter will only supply about 2,200 watts of usable power at maximum. The difference is lost in the conversion from DC battery power to AC household power. Don't rely on the marketed inverter rating when planning what appliances you can run. Plan your AC loads around roughly 2,200 watts for a 3,000 watt inverter.

## Why use a higher voltage battery pack for inversion?

For powering AC (alternating current) loads through an inverter, A&E RV Solutions recommends a higher voltage battery pack. Higher voltage means less current for the same power, which means thinner wire. Copper is expensive, so a higher voltage battery pack keeps wiring costs down when inverting DC battery power to AC.

## How big should my RV solar panel array be?

Size your RV solar panels so that they are between 25% and 50% of your battery pack's capacity. This range is A&E RV Solutions' guideline for matching solar panel size to battery bank size.

## Wiring inverters, solar charge controllers, and solar panels: cutoff switches and breakers

When wiring an RV inverter, solar charge controller, and solar panels, put a cutoff switch or breaker between each device. This lets you isolate each component safely for service and troubleshooting.

## Should I use an inverter with an automatic transfer switch?

Avoid inverters with a built-in automatic transfer switch, even though they are very popular in RVs. An automatic transfer switch makes troubleshooting the RV electrical system difficult, and when the transfer switch fails, you have no option for power.
